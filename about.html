LUNAR LANDER / POLICY LAB

Who can stick the landing?

Compare a human pilot with a PPO-trained AI, then take over whenever you want. The experiment is about how the policy flies—and how human intervention changes the flight.

From course exercise to cockpit

This lab builds on the Hugging Face Deep RL Course Unit 1 hands-on. The PPO checkpoint used here follows its LunarLander training-and-publishing workflow. This page runs that published checkpoint in the browser alongside a human pilot and a manual-override mode.

01 / FLIGHT MODES

Choose how to explore.

Human Pilot gives you direct control. AI Pilot lets the trained policy choose every action. Autopilot with Manual Override lets the AI fly until you intervene. 4 AI Simulations runs four independent policy-controlled flights with separate rewards and outcomes.

02 / THE POLICY

Eight values in. One action out.

The PPO policy normally receives the lander’s current eight-value observation and chooses among four actions: coast, rotate left, fire the main engine, or rotate right. With Sensor Fault enabled, an AI decision may receive the previous physics-step observation instead. Thruster Fault can drop a requested thrust action or fire a thruster when coast was requested; it applies to human and AI commands. Both browser-only fault settings default to OFF, and telemetry continues to show the true state.

03 / READING A FLIGHT

A flight is one sample.

Watch the lander, episode reward, and outcome together. The course tutorial evaluates across ten deterministic episodes; this page shows one interactive flight or four independent AI flights using a different physics solver and landing-completion rule. Fault settings are browser experiments, not part of the trained checkpoint or course evaluation. Treat each reward as an observation, not a reproduction of the course result.

Implementation details & credits

The environment and physics run in Python through Pyodide 0.23.4. PyPyBox2D supplies the pure-Python rigid-body solver; a small Gymnasium-compatible shim provides the local environment interface. JavaScript draws the canvas, handles keyboard input, and runs ONNX inference using ONNX Runtime Web 1.19.2 with its WebAssembly CPU backend.

The port follows the classic LunarLander-v2 observation and reward structure but is not bit-for-bit identical to the native Box2D environment. It uses PyPyBox2D’s older Box2D 2.1-era solver and adds a settled-contact landing completion rule.

Physics engine: PyPyBox2D by Ken Lauer, from the 2.1-r331 PyPI source release. The wheel retains the upstream zlib license notice.
